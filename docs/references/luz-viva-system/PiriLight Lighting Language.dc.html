Light that behaves like a living thing.

Every direction below is built from the same three-layer optical stack — a near-white luminous core, a soft icy-blue inner light, a deep desaturated atmospheric field — and the same organic breath curve: fast intake, long slow release, never symmetrical, never looping on a machine beat. What differs is the physics: how the light moves through space, how it touches matter, and what it leaves behind.

Respiração — the breathing ember

The light is a body, not a beam. It inhales in a fifth of a second and exhales for four — the asymmetry is the signature. The atmosphere around it always lags one beat behind the core, so the glow appears to have mass and warmth. Pollen-scale motes lift on the light's own convection.

Orvalho — light held in water

The signature is refraction, not emission. The source is a suspended droplet of light with surface tension — it wobbles when it moves and settles like liquid. Everything it touches receives slow caustic patterns, the way dawn light behaves through dew. Reveals arrive as a rising tide of illumination from below, never as a spotlight from above.

Direction 03

Névoa — light you can see the air in

Here the medium is the subject: the beam is only visible because of the dust it passes through. Three parallax haze planes give real depth — the nearest is out of focus, the farthest almost gone. Reveals happen with a single soft sheet of light drawn across an object, like silk pulled off furniture. Cinematic and patient.

Rules that hold across all three
Never symmetrical

Intake is always 10–20× faster than release. Symmetric pulsing reads as machinery.

The core is tiny

Under 12px, near-white, no colour. All the blue lives in the falloff, never in the source.

Three shells, no gradients

Core, inner ice, atmosphere — each with its own timing. Linear gradients are banned as light.

It always leaves a trace

Nothing snaps off. Every state decays into a faint residue the eye half-remembers.
